CLARITY OVER KEYWORD STUFFING.

A little guidance. A stronger story.

Understand the feedback, then make it your own.

01

Start with positioning

Write what you do, who you help, and what value you bring. Target role and audience fields are planning notes; they do not affect the score.

Try a headline structure:

[Role] | [Speciality] | Helping [audience] achieve [outcome]

For your About section, use a clear opening, relevant expertise, a truthful example, and an invitation to connect. Never invent credentials or results.

02

Understand the score

The Writing Quality Score is a transparent rules-based editing aid. It does not predict search visibility, hiring outcomes, or LinkedIn performance.

  • Length · 25%: 100 when non-empty and within your configured limit; otherwise 0.
  • Keyword coverage · 30%: percentage of selected keywords found at least once.
  • Readability · 25%: starts at 100; deducts 20 per sentence over 30 words and 25 per paragraph over 80 words.
  • Specific language · 20%: deducts 20 per flagged generic phrase and 25 per excessively repeated selected keyword.

Each check bottoms out at 0. With no keywords, that check is excluded and remaining weights are rescaled. Empty text scores 0. The overall score averages headline and About scores equally.

Rules are deliberately simple and can misread abbreviations, unusual formatting, or writing style. A high score is not proof of a persuasive bio.

03

Read keyword signals

Matching is case-insensitive and uses complete Unicode word tokens. Multiword phrases match adjacent words, not substrings. Punctuation between words is ignored.

Density = keyword phrase occurrences ÷ total words × 100. Each matched phrase counts as one occurrence, not the number of words in the phrase. Overlapping selected phrases are counted independently.

A repetition warning appears when a term occurs at least three times and exceeds 5% density. This is an editing heuristic, not a platform threshold. There is no “ideal” density target.

The generic-phrase list is intentionally small: guru, ninja, rockstar, results-driven, thought leader, and synergy. These are suggestions, not forbidden words.

04

Save with confidence

Your active working copy autosaves locally. “Save draft” creates or updates a named version in Saved Drafts. New draft starts a fresh version. Editing a saved draft updates it only when you choose Save draft.

JSON backups contain your saved drafts, not an unsaved working copy, settings, or theme. Imports accept at most 500 drafts and 5 MB, merge by ID, and replace matching IDs after confirmation.

No account, analytics, LinkedIn connection, AI service, or server database is used. Browser storage is not encrypted; shared-device users may see it. Clearing site data can delete drafts. Export regularly.

Clipboard and downloads depend on browser permissions. ES modules require HTTP hosting; opening the HTML through file:// is not supported.

Preview & platform limitations

Desktop and mobile previews are illustrative profile layouts, not exact LinkedIn replicas. “See more” uses a 320-character preview cutoff for demonstration only. Character counters count Unicode code points; emoji sequences may use several, and LinkedIn may count differently.

Headline and About limits default to 220 and 2,600 characters. These are configurable reference values, not live-verified platform rules. Check current LinkedIn limits before publishing. Nothing is posted automatically.
